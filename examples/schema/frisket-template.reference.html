The Frisket template

What a template may hold and what each part does. This page is generated from the model itself - its types, its own comments, and the engine answering - so it cannot say something the code does not. It is the readable face of the JSON schema beside it, which says the same things in a form an editor can check as you type.

It describes shape and meaning, not soundness. Whether a figure has alternative text, whether a table's rows agree in width, whether a template will survive being flattened - those are questions for frisket check, which reads a template and answers them.

The word field means three unrelated things in this model, and the page keeps them apart: a form control on the page, a path into the data, and a constant declared on the report.

Tokens

What a token in a text resolves to. Every cell below is the engine answering, on one made-up document: sheet 3 of 12, page 1 of 4 in its section, titled Quarterly report, generated on 2026-03-09 at 14:05, with one field called Customer.

The sheet this text is being drawn on, counted across the whole file from one. Backs are not counted: an invoice reading page 1 of 2 has four printed faces, and the reader counts what is written on the front.

Sheets in the whole file. Not known until pagination has finished, so a same-width placeholder stands in during layout and the real total is written into the finished fragments afterwards - which is why nothing moves and nothing rewraps, and why an advanced text cannot have it corrected.

{SectionPage}1a number format: {SectionPage:000} gives 001however it is speltleft for the resolver

Counting within one section rather than across the file, from one at each section boundary. Equal to {Page} while a report has one section, so a template may use it without knowing whether the report will ever gain a second.

This summary said the opposite until 24 August 2026 - that {Page} and {Pages} "keep meaning the whole section". They mean the whole file, and always have. It is what the Document-to-Section rename of 27 July did to a sentence where the word meant the file, which is the third thing CLAUDE.md warns a mass rename does and the last of them still standing.

Elements

Every type a template can hold, in the order the model reaches them from the report. A property is described where it is declared and linked from every kind that inherits it - more than half of what the schema states is that repetition.

Report

The whole template, and the one file that comes out of it: the properties a reader and an archive see - title, author, language - the defaults every item inherits from, the mark and the back printed on every sheet, and the list of sections the content is laid out on.

Everything here that is not about the file names the first section only. Dimension, Header, Body and Footer are conveniences over Sections[0], so once there is a second section the page size set through the report still reaches only the first, the new one keeps the sheet of nothing it was born with, and items added through report.Body go on landing where they always did.

LineSpacinga number?null

Distance from one baseline to the next, as a multiple of the text's own size - that is, of its cap height, the thing a size in millimetres actually pins. Defaults to 1.5.

Deliberately not a multiple of the em, and deliberately not the font's own line height: both vary by half from one typeface to the next, so a table row would change height simply because the section was restyled. BackPage

What is printed on the back of a sheet - conditions of sale, most often.

It holds ordinary items, so it can be typed text with{tokens}, an image of a page, or any mix. It is laid out on a full page of its own: no header, no footer and no page band, because a back is not part of the document's flow.

Back pages are not numbered and not counted. An invoice reading "page 1 / 2" has two sides of content and four printed faces, and the reader counts what is written on the front.{Page}on a back therefore gives the number of the sheet it belongs to, which is what makes "conditions applying to page 2" possible.

Watermark

A word written across every page - SPECIMEN, BROUILLON, COPIE.

It is drawn as ordinary page content, not as a background. That distinction is the whole point: a browser drops background graphics when printing unless the reader goes and turns them back on, so a mark drawn as a background is exactly absent from the one artefact that matters - the printout someone might pass off as an original.

The engine draws it and takes no view on whether a given section needs one.

Section "$type": "Section"

One section of a report: a page setup and the bands laid out on it.

A report holds a *list* of these, in order, which is what lets one file mix an A4 portrait body with an A3 landscape appendix - and what lets separate sections be assembled into one. PDF works the same way: the page size is

/MediaBoxon each page object, not a property of the file.

**Nothing flows from one document into the next.** Content that does not fit adds pages within its own section; it never spills into the following one. That is a deliberate limit rather than an omission: it makes the one genuinely hard case - a table splitting between two page sizes and changing width mid-continuation - inexpressible rather than merely discouraged.

A `Section` inside a `Body` means nothing and cannot be drawn. The type system permits it because a document is an item like any other; the validator is what forbids it.

ContainerItem "$type": "ContainerItem"

A group of items, placed either at coordinates of their own or stacked one under the next. Everything in the model that holds children is one of these - the bands, a table and its rows, an image grid, a section.

An absolute container hands every child the same box rather than taking room away as it goes, and a child that states no position of its own fills all of it: two that say nothing land exactly on top of one another. Nor does it ever continue onto the next page - it is placed whole, and runs off the sheet if it must. Which containers avoid that by flowing is measured in test 70, not remembered here.

BaseItem "$type": "BaseItem"

Anything a template can put on a page. Every element is one of these, and what it declares here is what they all have in common whatever their kind: where the item sits and how it looks (Style), what it is for a reader who cannot see it (Role, AltText), whether it earns its place at all (ShowWhen, HideWhen), and where it lands in the outline (Bookmark).

An item sits in exactly one container. Adding one that already has a Parent does nothing and returns false - a result nothing in this repository reads - so a rule put in the header and then in the footer appears in the header and is silently missing from the footer. Clone() is what makes the second one, which is why every place that moves an item somewhere else clones it first.
